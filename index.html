Editing happens on the protospacer strand, so the tool picks the guide strand for you: an A or C you click is edited by a sense-strand guide, a T or G by an antisense guide.

Adenine transversion editing is unpublished.

The zAYE parameters here come from a manuscript in preparation (Qin, Zhang, Huang & Varshney) and may change before publication. Two features make these editors behave unlike the transition editors above:

  • The window is narrow — positions 6–8 only, peaking at 7. Far fewer sites are reachable, so PAM availability is the main constraint.
  • Products are mixed. Deamination leaves an abasic intermediate that cellular repair resolves several ways, so one edited base yields A→C and A→T together, plus residual A→G and some indels. At the two disease variants reported, A→C was predominant at about 31–33%, with roughly 10% A→T and 10–18% A→G alongside it. Plan genotyping accordingly: the intended allele is a fraction of the edited alleles, not all of them.

Off-target checking

This page does not search the genome — that needs the GRCz11 reference and an index. Copy a spacer and run it through CRISPOR or Cas-OFFinder against Danio rerio GRCz11/danRer11, which is what all three Umax studies used (up to four mismatches, then amplicon sequencing of the top-ranked sites). Efficiency predictions from BE-Hive or DeepBE are also out of scope: those are trained models, and neither was trained on zebrafish embryos.

Window placement and PAM rules come from the published selection strategies. Ranking is a heuristic for triaging guides, not a trained efficiency prediction — the papers are clear that locus behaviour varies and several candidates are worth testing in parallel.
